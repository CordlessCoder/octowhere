# Hardware topology notes

- The secondary PCB is connected to the WaveShare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75 through both the expansion header and the five pads immediately beside that header.
- The secondary PCB's J3 pin 3 `3v3` net is the LoRa module VCC rail. It mates with WaveShare H2 pin 3, which the WaveShare schematic labels `VCC3V3`; that rail is the AXP2101 DCDC1 output.
- The WaveShare schematic places the I²C pull-ups on the main board: R23 and R24 are 2.2 kΩ from `VCC3V3` to `ESP32_SCL` and `ESP32_SDA`. The secondary PCB has no I²C pull-ups; its schematic explicitly says “no need for i2c pullups”.
- The bench capture measured the LoRa module's VCC pin directly at about 1 V when the digital SDA and SCL traces transitioned high. This is a measurement of the secondary board's `3v3`/LoRa supply, not an inference from the I²C waveform. Since that rail and the main-board I²C pull-ups share `VCC3V3`, the bus high may have been tracking an underpowered rail. A digital analyzer high is only a threshold classification, however; the capture does not measure the SDA/SCL amplitudes or establish whether the rail was still ramping, had collapsed under load, or was being clamped by an unpowered peripheral.
- If the physical SDA/SCL high level at the secondary board was also near 1 V, that voltage is below both relevant guarantees: the AXP2101 specifies a 1.3 V minimum high level on its TWSI pins, and the SX1272 specifies a 1.8 V minimum supply. In that case the bus high is not valid for the PMIC and the LoRa module is not within its operating range. The measurement is at the secondary board, so it does not by itself prove that the AXP output pin or the ESP32 supply was also at 1 V; a local drop or load-induced collapse remains possible.
- The ESP32-S3's `VDD3P3`, `VDD3P3_CPU`, and `VDD3P3_RTC` supplies are specified for 3.0–3.6 V, and Espressif requires the rails to stabilize before `CHIP_PU` is released. Because the ESP32-S3 and LoRa VCC share `VCC3V3`, a 1 V LoRa measurement cannot represent normal firmware execution on that same rail unless the rail is collapsing locally. If the first SDA/SCL rise precedes any SCL edge, it is likely passive rail-ramp activity; if a transaction is already running, the capture shows a power-integrity or reset-timing failure rather than merely an I²C driver ordering issue.
- The secondary PCB powers the BMM350 VDDIO and its I²C interface from `3v3`, while its core VDD comes from the local TPS7A2018 1.8 V regulator. Bosch allows either supply to rise first, but the sensor remains powered off until both exceed their reset-release thresholds. Its documented OFF-to-suspend startup time is 2.5 ms, so the sensor does not explain a required 250 ms delay by specification; it can still be a load or an incompletely powered I²C participant during the rail ramp.
- The CST9217 reference specifies 100 ms for power-on initialization and 100 ms for reinitialization after reset. The touch driver now waits 100 ms before its first transaction and after releasing the reset pin. The full firmware initializes touch about 1 s after I²C setup, so this was a separate driver defect, not the current PMIC/display cold-boot boundary.
- The CST9217 reports a covered panel for as long as the hand stays, up to about 180 ms apart, and does not reliably report the hand lifting. It also sends unreadable reports during a cover. `docs/logs/touch/` has the recording.
- A read before the CST9217 writes its next report returns the host's own acknowledgement (`0xab`) in byte 0. A still finger is reported less often than the frame loop polls, so these arrive mid-contact and must not count as a lift. A finger lift is followed first by a report with `0x00` in byte 0. `docs/logs/touch/finger-hold-lift-2026-09-24.log` has the recording.
- If SCL clock edges occurred while this shared rail was near 1 V, the 3.3 V peripherals were outside their guaranteed supply and I²C input ranges. Capture `PWROK`/`CHIP_PU` with the rail and the first SCL edge to distinguish a premature reset release from a rail collapse after the ESP32-S3 started. SDA/SCL rising without clock edges would instead be the passive pull-ups following the rail.
- The cold-boot failure boundary points to delaying I²C initialization until `VCC3V3` has settled. A cold-boot binary search found 42 ms unreliable and 43 ms working; 31, 38, and 42 ms failed, while 44, 46, 62, 125, and 250 ms recovered the display. The firmware uses 80 ms, providing a 37 ms margin over the minimum observed working delay. The exact electrical mechanism still needs confirmation with a capture of the rail, `PWROK`/`CHIP_PU`, and the first I²C transaction.
- With USB power and no battery fitted, the PMIC reports about 5080 mV on VBUS and 3750 mV on VSYS. The VBAT ADC value varies without a battery, so the UI must use the PMIC battery-presence result rather than display that raw value as a battery voltage.
- The five adjacent WaveShare pads are, from the board reference: TP3 = SDA / `ESP32_SDA`, TP2 = SCL / `ESP32_SCL`, TP4 = EXIO0, TP5 = EXIO1, and TP6 = EXIO2.
- The WaveShare H2 8-pin header is a separate connection: H2-1=`VBUS`, H2-2=`GND`, H2-3=`VCC3V3`, H2-4=`U0TXD`/GPIO43, H2-5=`U0RXD`/GPIO44, H2-6=GPIO16, H2-7=GPIO17, and H2-8=GPIO18. The secondary PCB J3 mates to this header in the same pin order.
- The trace from the WaveShare board reference is: TP3 → `ESP32_SDA` → TCA9554 SDA and GNSS/RTC I²C; TP2 → `ESP32_SCL` → TCA9554 SCL and GNSS/RTC I²C; TP4 → `EXIO0`; TP5 → `EXIO1`; TP6 → `EXIO2`. These are not generic GPIOs. TP4–TP6 are TCA9554 expander outputs, and TP2/TP3 are the shared I²C bus.
- On the secondary PCB, the named test pads are TP3 → `i2c_sda`, TP2 → `ex0`, TP4 → `ex1`, and TP5 → `ex2`; `i2c_scl` is present as a routed net but has no matching secondary-PCB test-point footprint. The five-pad connection must therefore be treated as a board-to-board contact set, not inferred from the secondary PCB test-point reference designators. The expansion connector separately carries the named J3 signals listed below.
- The secondary PCB uses the shared I²C lines through TP3 and TP2. Its BMM350 INT is marked unconnected in the secondary PCB layout, so it is not available through this five-pad connection.
- Do not infer secondary-PCB signal availability from the expansion-header schematic alone. Trace those five adjacent pads in the WaveShare board reference before deciding whether a secondary-PCB signal is unavailable to firmware.
- The secondary-PCB schematic is `/home/me/git/octowhere-pcb/Compass_extender.kicad_sch`; the WaveShare board reference is `/home/me/git/octowhere-pcb/ESP32 S3 Touch AMOLED 1.75.pdf`.
- The WaveShare reference routes native USB D−/D+ to ESP32-S3 GPIO19/GPIO20 (`USB_N`/`USB_P`). GPIO43 and GPIO44 are instead the UART0 signals `U0TXD` and `U0RXD`. The LoRa suspicion is therefore not native USB data interference. It remains possible for GPIO44 to be electrically affected by an external UART connection or another board function using `U0RXD`; the reference must be checked for any populated UART transceiver or pull/load on that net.
- The WaveShare schematic connects AXP2101 `PWROK` (pin 29) to the ESP32-S3 `CHIP_PU` net. The exposed board test point is labelled `PWROK`/`RST`. Any PMIC condition that pulls `PWROK` low can therefore reset or hold the ESP32-S3, so PMIC register writes must be treated as reset-path operations, not only power telemetry.
- The WaveShare component-side layout identifies TP11 as the exposed `RST` pad for the `PWROK` net and TP1 beside it as `GND`. The main-board VCC3V3 probe is the H2 header pad silk-labelled `3V3`; the reference does not show a separate dedicated VCC3V3 test pad. Probe the main-board side of that solder joint if the secondary board covers the header.
- The WaveShare 8-pin header maps pin 4 to `U0TXD`/GPIO43, pin 5 to `U0RXD`/GPIO44, pin 6 to GPIO16, pin 7 to GPIO17, and pin 8 to GPIO18. The secondary PCB J3 netlist maps pin 4 to `LoRa_MOSI`, pin 5 to `LoRa_DIO0`, pin 6 to `IO16`, pin 7 to `IO17`, and pin 8 to `IO18`. Therefore the assembled PCB mapping is MOSI=GPIO43, DIO0=GPIO44, SCLK=GPIO16, MISO=GPIO17, and NSS=GPIO18. The previous firmware used GPIO44 for MOSI, which was one header contact wrong.
- The onboard LC76G GNSS module uses the shared `ESP32_SDA`/`ESP32_SCL` bus and is reset through TCA9554 `EXIO7` (`GPS_RST`). The board reference marks the alternate GPS UART routes as `NC/GPS_TXD` and `NC/GPS_RXD`, so the firmware uses the GNSS I²C interface.
- The LC76G I²C protocol uses address `0x50` for configuration commands, `0x54` for NMEA reads, and `0x58` for NMEA writes. The module can NACK while its internal buffers change; the Quectel application note specifies 10 ms delays and retries, which the driver implements.
- The Quectel I²C sample retries each complete protocol step: configuration write, delay, and data transfer. Retrying only the `0x54` read is insufficient because the LC76G transmitter can enter a sleep state when its buffer is not serviced; a new configuration command wakes and re-arms that path. The driver now retries the complete sequence for NMEA reads and writes. A live failure showed GNSS `0x54` address NACKs while PMIC and BMM350 transactions continued to work, ruling out a total shared-bus failure. After the change, the receiver again produced continuous NMEA data and 3D fixes during monitoring.
- LoRa link-test builds had accidentally skipped the one-second LC76G startup settle delay. Their first configuration write reached `0x50` about 23 ms after reset release and was NACKed, while the other I²C devices remained responsive. The settle delay now applies to every build; subsequent LoRa transmitter and receiver runs produced NMEA data without GNSS I²C failures.
- The `lc76g` crate provides a bounded checksummed `PairCommandBuilder` and generic `PairMessage` response events, so commands not yet given a typed convenience method remain configurable without hand-written checksum or buffer code.
- The firmware sends `$PAIR080,0*2E`, `$PAIR050,1000*12`, and `$PAIR732,1*21` after reset to request Adaptive Low Power (ALP) mode with its documented normal-navigation and 1 Hz prerequisites. The module's `$PAIR001` acknowledgements are parsed as typed events; unsupported commands remain non-fatal because older LC76G firmware revisions do not support every command.
- The firmware enables one-hertz GSA and GSV output for acquisition diagnostics. The GNSS parser exposes fix dimensionality, dilution values, satellites used, satellites in view, satellites with SNR, and strongest observed SNR. Build with `--features gnss-full-power` to send the ALP disable command and leave the receiver in normal tracking mode during acquisition tests.
- WaveShare's maintained `HARDWARE_REFERENCE.md` assigns the TCA9554 inputs as P3=`RTC_INT`, P4=`SYS_OUT`, P5=`AXP_IRQ`, and P6=`QMI_INT1`; P7 is `GPS_RST`. The current firmware leaves those inputs as inputs and only drives P0 and P7 for the secondary-board resets.
- WaveShare's maintained Brookesia firmware starts the CO5300 display before starting its background AXP2101 status monitor. That monitor probes the PMIC and then changes TS measurement and charger fields. This is a known-good startup ordering, although the standalone AXP2101 example initializes the PMIC before its display, so the ordering is evidence rather than a universal requirement.
- WaveShare's maintained Brookesia source at commit `e4344e7` keeps AXP2101 probing out of fatal startup: it starts the display and application first, then adds the PMIC to the existing I²C bus, removes a failed device, and retries after five monitor cycles. The current Rust path instead unwraps `power.init()` before the display starts. A transient early PMIC failure can therefore leave the panel dark even if later PMIC access would succeed.
- Observed on 2026-09-24: `power.init()` returned an I²C error, and the firmware panicked at that
  unwrap, on at least 5 of about 30 boots directly after `espflash flash`. Each time the next
  flash of the same image booted normally. The failures were on the first PMIC transaction after
  reset, so the transient early failure described above does happen in practice.
- The same BSP's I²C setup only binds GPIO15/SDA and GPIO14/SCL to the ESP-IDF master. It does not perform an initial transaction, add software pull-ups, or configure the PMIC while creating the bus. The Rust path uses the same pins and the same external-pull-up topology, but performs PMIC reads immediately and treats `power.init()` failure as fatal.
- TCA9554 direction register bits are 1 for inputs and 0 for outputs. During reset release, keep only P0 as an output (`0xFE` direction) if P7 is released to its board pull-up; `0x7F` would accidentally drive P1-P6, including `AXP_IRQ`, `SYS_OUT`, and the sensor interrupt lines.
- AXP2101 `IRQ` is an open-drain, active-low output connected to TCA9554 P5/`AXP_IRQ`. The AXP2101 datasheet also documents IRQ-low as a configurable power-on source after 16 ms. Driving P5 high from the TCA9554 can therefore contend with a PMIC interrupt and alter the PMIC power state; this makes the old `0x7F` direction write a plausible cold-reconnect trigger rather than only an input-sampling error.
- The AXP2101 datasheet says that driving `PWROK` low restarts the PMU. The enable is `REG10[3]` and its power-on default is disabled. Because the board routes `PWROK` to `CHIP_PU`, any PMIC reset-path event can reset the ESP32-S3 even when the 3.3 V rail stays within range. The current driver does not write `REG10`, so it does not intentionally enable this reset mode.
- The secondary LoRa schematic PDF has the UART-header signal labels shifted relative to the actual connector pin order. The PCB netlist makes J3 pin 4 `LoRa_MOSI` and J3 pin 5 `LoRa_DIO0`; with the WaveShare header order, those are GPIO43 and GPIO44 respectively.
- The secondary PCB file assigns U4 RESET to `ex0`, nSEL to `IO18`, SCLK to `IO16`, SDO to `IO17`, and SDI to `LoRa_MOSI`/J3 pin 4. This PCB mapping is confirmed: MOSI=GPIO43, MISO=GPIO17, SCLK=GPIO16, NSS=GPIO18, DIO0=GPIO44.
- The secondary PCB routes the RF switch controls as `ex1` → module `RX_SWITCH` (pin 4) and `ex2` → module `TX_SWITCH` (pin 5). Both nets have pull-ups to 3v3. For receive, the module datasheet requires `RX_SWITCH=1` and `TX_SWITCH=0`; for transmit it requires `RX_SWITCH=0` and `TX_SWITCH=1`. Leaving TCA9554 P1/P2 as inputs therefore leaves both controls high and selects neither valid path. The board integration must drive P1/P2 as outputs and change them with the radio mode.
- The SX1272/73 V2b errata identifies `RegVersion=0x22`, requires bit 7 of `RegDetectOptimize` (`0x31`) to be cleared after reset to reduce spurious reception, and says not to rely on the valid-packet counter. The forked driver applies the receive workaround during initialization; applications should count successful packets from `RxDone` and CRC status rather than use the silicon counter.
- SX1272/73 reset is active-high. The firmware pulses `EXIO0` high for 200 µs and releases it before SPI access.
- The earlier firmware used GPIO44 for MOSI, one header position wrong, so all register reads were zero. After changing MOSI to GPIO43 and DIO0 to GPIO44, the board returned `RegOpMode=0x01`, `RegFrfMsb=0xE4`, `RegIrqFlags=0x15`, and `RegVersion=0x22`; the radio is now identified correctly.
- CO5300 TE, measured on 2026-09-24 (`bench/tearing`): in its default mode (`0x35` with 0) TE is high through the vertical blanking, 0.38–0.63 ms of a 16.54–16.80 ms frame, so the panel scans its rows for about 16.1 ms. A full-panel flush took 14.0–16.6 ms while the other core drew full frames into PSRAM, about as long as the scan. Started at the blanking, the write raced the scan and tore on several lines at once wherever it fell behind; the owner saw it clearly during drags. With `0x44` set to line 150, TE becomes a pulse (1.11 ms high, 16.77–16.81 ms period) when the scan reaches that line. Flushing from its rising edge keeps the write behind the scan, and the owner saw no tearing. The lead must exceed the scan time less the shortest full flush, about 2.1 ms or 61 lines at the time.

## Pin map

GPIO numbers were removed from [`src/board.rs`](../src/board.rs) because esp-hal takes typed
peripheral singletons and a `u8` can never reach it. Two of those constants had already drifted
without anything noticing: `RTC_INT` claimed GPIO39, which is the display reset, and `TP_I2C_ADDR`
claimed `0x38`, the FT6236 address, where the CST9217 driver uses `0x5A`. The table below is
transcribed from the binding sites in `main.rs`, which are the live record.

| Signal | Pin | Bound at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LCD SCLK | GPIO38 | `with_sck` |
| LCD SIO0–SIO3 | GPIO4, GPIO5, GPIO6, GPIO7 | `with_sio0`–`with_sio3` |
| LCD CS | GPIO12 | `Output` into `QspiBus` |
| LCD TE | GPIO13 | `Input` into `Co5300Display` |
| LCD reset | GPIO39 | `Output` into `Co5300Display` |
| I2C SDA / SCL | GPIO15 / GPIO14 | `with_sda` / `with_scl` |
| Touch reset / INT | GPIO40 / GPIO11 | `touch_rst` / `touch_int` |
| IMU INT2 | GPIO21 | `imu_int2` |
| LoRa SCLK / MISO / MOSI / NSS / DIO0 | GPIO16 / GPIO17 / GPIO43 / GPIO18 / GPIO44 | LoRa SPI setup |

The IMU has two interrupt lines: INT1 reaches the TCA9554 as `EXIO_QMI_INT1`, INT2 is GPIO21. The
RTC interrupt is on the expander as `EXIO_RTC_INT`, not a GPIO.

Unbound in firmware, recorded from the Waveshare `pin_config.h` and not verified against this
board: SD CLK/CMD/DATA GPIO2/GPIO1/GPIO3 with CS GPIO41, boot button GPIO0, power button GPIO10,
and the ES8311 audio codec. No firmware drives any of these.
- Both motion sensors sit face down and turned against the screen. In the screen's axes (x toward the right edge, y toward the top edge, z out of the glass), the QMI8658 reads screen = (y, x, −z) and the BMM350 reads screen = (−x, −y, z). These come from twelve held poses captured with the axis check screen and fitted by `tools/fit-sensor-axes.py`, both since removed and kept in git history: every other axis mapping fitted at least 40 times worse for the IMU, and at 21.5 µT RMS against 3.2 µT for the magnetometer. The same capture put the board's own field at about (−2.1, −59.5, 4.0) µT in the BMM350's axes, and the field at the capture site at 8.8 µT horizontal and 42.9 µT vertical, indoors.
- The QMI8658 gyro reads about 0.32 rad/s (18°/s) on its y axis at rest, with under 0.05 rad/s on the other two. It was the same across three sessions, with a sample spread under 0.015 rad/s, so it is an offset rather than noise. `ui::fusion` measures it whenever the board is still and subtracts it. The part reports revision 0x7C, a QMI8658A. Its on-demand calibration (CTRL9 command 0xA2) completes in 1.5 s and reports success, but settles the offset at about (0, 8, 0.5)°/s from (2.6, 18.2, 0.3)°/s, and a reset undoes it. The `ph-qmi8658` driver's `run_on_demand_calibration` waits only 10 ms for the command, so it returns `NotReady` and leaves the sensors disabled. The firmware does not use it; the bench is `bench/gyro-cod`, run with `cargo build --release --features gyro-cod-bench` and flashed with the board still.
